Nina Andrejevic

Assistant Computational Scientist • Argonne National Laboratory

Hi! I'm an Assistant Computational Scientist at Argonne National Laboratory working at the intersection of machine learning and materials characterization. My research focuses on developing physics-aware deep learning models to assist the analysis of X-ray scattering and spectroscopic measurements of nanoscale materials. Alongside my research, I'm also enthusiastic about science communication through teaching and scientific data visualization.

Data Visualization

Optical absorption palette for metal oxides. Sunburst plots of the optical absorbance spectra for representative metal oxides, grouped by constituent element and composition. Color encodes absorbance intensity across energy while angular sectors denote compositional variation. Visualization of the dataset from Stein et al., Scientific Data 6, 9 (2019). In collaboration with Jovana Andrejevic Kim.
Interstitial network bottleneck radii for Li-containing compounds. A periodic-table–based summary of the average interstitial bottleneck radius RT in Li-containing materials, where each element's annulus encodes the mean threshold radius and its variability (±1σ) across compounds; colors indicate RT, and the histogram shows the distribution stratified by interstitial network dimensionality. Visualization of the dataset from He et al., Scientific Data 7, 153 (2020). In collaboration with Jovana Andrejevic Kim.
Streams of thought in Nobel laureates' scientific writing. Streamgraphs trace the temporal evolution of frequently used terms in publication titles by Nobel Prize winners across chemistry, physics, and medicine from the late 19th century to the present; stream width reflects word frequency, color denotes the time of first occurrence, and annotated branches highlight prominent concepts. Visualization of the dataset from Li et al., Scientific Data 6, 33 (2019). In collaboration with Jovana Andrejevic Kim.
